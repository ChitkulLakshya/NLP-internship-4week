## Week 1 — Exploratory Data Analysis & Preprocessing in NLP (312 words)

This report presents a complete exploratory data analysis and preprocessing study on a 1,500-document mixed text corpus proxying Amazon reviews, Sentiment140 tweets, and AG-News snippets (sources: twitter/amazon/news with ratings 1–5, dates 2023–2024). The pipeline in src/preprocessing.py and src/eda.py audits missingness (3.1%), noisy patterns (URLs, mentions, hashtags, HTML, emojis, elongations — 11.4% noisy), document-length distributions by source, source and rating balances, top-30 terms and top-20 bi/trigrams, and a lexicon sentiment proxy. Eight figures (length histogram, source/rating bars, top-term/bigram bars, sentiment bars, noise audit, length-by-source boxplot) plus statistical tables support every claim. Cleaning lowercases, strips URLs/mentions/HTML, maps #word→word, preserves emojis as <EMOJI>, collapses elongations, and tokenizes with a zero-dependency regex tokenizer plus vendored stopwords and optional stemming — each choice justified by sparsity, sentiment-retention, and reproducibility arguments with ablations. Challenges (missing rows, hashtag/emoji handling, rating skew, length variance, domain mixing) and mitigations are documented, closing with five modeling insights that directly motivate the Week-2 TF-IDF 1–2gram + LinearSVC design: Zipf-validated vocab, discriminative bigrams, rating-sentiment correlation, noise-induced OOV control, and source as auxiliary signal. Reproducibility is guaranteed via seed 42, scripts/make_data.py (no network), and one-command regeneration of figures and metrics.

## Week 2 — Designing and Implementing Text Classification Strategies (305 words)

This report designs, implements, and validates a four-class text classifier (tech/sports/politics/entertainment) on 2,000 labelled news/review texts mirroring AG-News and IMDB phrasing. The shared Week-1 normalizer feeds TF-IDF vectors (max 5,000 features, 1–2grams, min_df 2) into three algorithms — Multinomial Naive Bayes, Linear Support Vector Machine, and Logistic Regression — chosen as complementary sparse-text baselines (generative vs max-margin vs calibrated discriminative). Evaluation uses stratified 80/20 hold-out plus five-fold cross-validation with accuracy, macro-F1 as primary metric, per-class precision/recall, and confusion matrices for every model. LinearSVC achieves the best held-out macro-F1 around 0.94, followed closely by Logistic Regression, with Naive Bayes two to three points lower due to violated independence on correlated bigrams; CV standard deviations under 0.02 confirm stability. Class-balance, model-comparison, and three confusion-matrix figures expose the main error mode (politics↔tech overlap on policy/startup vocabulary). Feature ablations, training cost (<10s CPU), validation rationale, and five concrete enhancements — domain-lexicon features, character n-grams, calibrated thresholds, class-weighted SVM for skewed live data, and a DistilBERT upgrade path with active learning — are discussed with cost-benefit analysis. All code lives in src/classification.py, fully seeded and tested, with figures and metrics regenerable via scripts/run_all.py, making the strategy immediately transferable to real AG-News or client ticket-routing data.

## Week 3 — Sentiment Analysis and Named Entity Recognition (318 words)

This dual mini-project report implements sentiment analysis and named entity recognition on a single 1,200-document product/blog corpus with gold sentiment labels and PERSON/ORG/LOC/DATE/MONEY spans, mirroring IMDB polarity and CoNLL-2003 NER schemas. Preprocessing reuses the Week-1 pipeline with a critical split: lowercased input for sentiment, raw cased input for NER. The sentiment track contrasts an interpretable lexicon voter (accuracy ~0.74) against TF-IDF plus Logistic Regression (accuracy ~0.92, macro-F1 ~0.92), showing via confusion matrices and per-class tables that bigrams like highly recommend and total waste drive the 18-point gain while neutral remains hardest and negation/sarcasm bound the lexicon. The NER track contributes a transparent rule-based extractor — person bigrams, org-suffix patterns, city gazetteer, date and money regexes with longest-first overlap resolution — scored by exact-match micro precision/recall/F1 (~0.78 F1) with a predicted-type histogram and error breakdown proving high precision on rigid DATE/MONEY patterns and gazetteer-limited recall on LOC. Preprocessing, feature, and algorithm choices are justified against alternatives (VADER, sklearn-CRF, spaCy, BERT-NER), challenges including negation scope, sarcasm, neutral vagueness, brittle rules, and case sensitivity are each paired with mitigations, and a joint aspect-based sentiment extension is proposed. The modules in src/sentiment_ner.py are pytest-covered, seeded, and one-command reproducible, demonstrating both theoretical grounding and production-ready text-analytics practice.

## Week 4 — Advanced Analysis and Reporting of Language Trends (301 words)

This capstone report synthesizes all prior skills into a temporal language-trend study on 1,800 timestamped documents spanning January 2022 to December 2024, probing AI/tech emergence, covid decline, election cyclicality, and cricket, film, price, and climate baselines. The pipeline in src/trends.py applies Week-1 normalization, then TF-IDF plus KMeans clustering (k=6, silhouette reported) and Count plus Latent Dirichlet Allocation topic modeling (k=6, top-10 terms per topic listed) alongside monthly keyword-share curves and volume charts — four publication-ready figures anchor the narrative. Results reveal the dominant drift of ai from ~5% to ~27% document share, an inverse covid fall, bimodal election spikes around 2023–2024 polls, and stable seasonal entertainment baselines, with ~70% manual cluster-topic alignment confirming coherence despite modest silhouette typical of sparse text. Each method's rationale, hyperparameters, and trade-offs versus modern alternatives (BERTopic, UMAP/HDBSCAN, dynamic topic models, Kleinberg burst detection, change-point tests) are critically evaluated, alongside newsroom, brand-monitoring, and public-health implications, explicit synthetic-data limitations, and a future-work roadmap toward interactive dashboards, causal analysis, and human topic-rating studies. The end-to-end workflow from timestamp parsing through trend visualization is fully seeded, tested, and reproducible with a single command, evidencing mature real-world NLP analytics ready for deployment on NYT or Guardian archives.
